# Sub-brand concept

Status: **draft for owner decision** (2026-10-19). Comes before any interface work. Builds on
`robinhood-system.md` and `thesis-positioning.md`.

---

## 1. Why a sub-brand at all

Robinhood is at the settled stage of the brand arc. It started as the risky alternative, won the
people willing to take a chance, then rebranded in 2024 for "serious investors" with restraint,
black and white, and "less is more". That brand is right for its matured, cautious base.

It's wrong for the people who want to hand their trading to an agent. Telling them "Robinhood now
has an agentic model" in a black-and-white, distraction-free wrapper sells them the brand they
already outgrew.

A sub-brand lets both stand:

- **Robinhood** keeps restraint for the Cautious Holder.
- **The sub-brand** speaks to people who want to take a chance on automation, with its own
  audience, voice and look.
- **Same behavioral system underneath.** Same mandates, limits, approvals and stop controls.

### Brand architecture: endorsed, not standalone

On Aaker's brand relationship spectrum, this should be an **endorsed brand** ("[Name], by
Robinhood"), not a house of brands.

- The endorsement carries trust (Safety Always) into a category where trust is the whole problem.
- The sub-brand gets freedom of tone, look and audience.
- Robinhood Gold and the Gold Card are precedent for a distinct sub-identity under the parent.

## 2. The audience, redefined

Age doesn't define this audience. **Appetite for automation** does, crossed with **trading
experience**.

```text
                     trading experience
                 new                    experienced
             ┌────────────────────┬────────────────────┐
 automation  │  THE APPRENTICE    │  THE OPERATOR      │
 appetite    │  "show me how"     │  "learn how I do   │
 high        │                    │   it, then run it" │
             ├────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
 automation  │  (core Robinhood)  │  (core Robinhood)  │
 appetite    │                    │                    │
 low         │   Cautious Holder — not this brand's audience
             └────────────────────┴────────────────────┘
```

### The Apprentice

New or newish to trading. Comfortable letting AI do things for them elsewhere: assistants,
recommendations, autopilot features. Doesn't want to learn markets from a textbook. Wants to learn
by doing, with someone watching.

- **Need:** guidance and confidence. "Walk me through it, and don't let me blow up."
- **The agent's role:** a **mentor**. It explains, proposes and shows its work. The user approves.
- **Autonomy starts low and grows as the user earns it.**

### The Operator

Already trades with a method: screens, entries, exits, a routine. Spends hours repeating it. Already
uses automation in other work (coding agents, scripts, recurring buys).

- **Need:** offload the grind without giving up the edge. "Learn my playbook, run it, tell me
  when it's off-script."
- **The agent's role:** a **sidekick**. It learns the user's patterns, then runs them inside limits
  the user sets.
- **Autonomy starts high, bounded by an explicit mandate.**

### The symmetry: who's teaching whom

- For the **Apprentice**, the agent teaches the human.
- For the **Operator**, the human teaches the agent.

Both are an **apprenticeship**, pointed in opposite directions. That one idea organizes the brand,
the onboarding and the autonomy model.

### A word on "high frequency"

Avoid it. In finance, high-frequency trading means institutional, microsecond-scale execution.
Retail agents don't do that, and the term invites the wrong scrutiny. Use **always-on** or
**hands-off** instead.

## 3. Brand concept

### Core idea: undefined, but held

The brand embraces what the parent brand edits out: possibility, motion, the unknown of handing
over control. But it's never loose. Control is always visible and always in reach.

> **Take the chance. Keep the leash.**

### Principles (contrast with the parent)

| Robinhood (parent) | Sub-brand                  | What it means                                                                         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Restraint          | **Range**                  | Show the possibility space, not just the next button                                   |
| Static focus       | **Alive**                  | The agent is working. The brand moves, because the agent is moving                     |
| Transaction        | **Relationship**           | Mentor or sidekick, never a vending machine                                             |
| Simplicity         | **Earned autonomy**        | Autonomy grows as trust is shown, in either direction                                   |
| Safety as policy   | **Control in reach**       | Pause, stop and limits are part of the brand's look, not a settings page               |

### The key move: the visual identity *is* agent state

This is where the brand and the thesis become the same argument. The repo's core claim is that
agentic systems must encode **autonomy, temporality, uncertainty and control state**. The
sub-brand's visual language encodes exactly those:

- **Form loosens with autonomy, tightens near limits.** Abstract, generative shapes that open up
  when the agent has room and draw in as it approaches a boundary. "Undefined, but held," made
  literal.
- **Motion means work.** Idle is still; working breathes; waiting for you pulses toward you.
- **Uncertainty has texture.** Low-confidence output looks less resolved than high-confidence output.
- **Control is solid.** Stop, pause and limit controls are the one fixed, unmoving element. They
  never animate away.

So the brand isn't decoration over the behavioral layer. It's the behavioral tokens, given a look.

### Guardrail: risk-embracing, never gamified

Robinhood removed confetti in 2021 after regulators cited it. This brand invites risk-takers, so
it has to be stricter than the parent here:

- No celebration of trades, gains or streaks.
- No slot-machine motion. Motion reflects agent state only, never reward.
- Losses are shown as plainly as gains.

## 4. Voice

| Parent (product register)        | Sub-brand                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Plain, short, neutral            | Plain, short, **direct and a little daring**                       |
| Second person                    | Second person, plus **the agent speaks for itself** ("I'll hold this for you") |
| Calm about risk                  | **Honest** about risk: names it, doesn't hide it, doesn't hype it  |

**Two dialects, one voice:**

- **To the Apprentice:** patient, explains why. "Here's what I'd do and why. Your call."
- **To the Operator:** terse, peer to peer. "Your setup hit at 9:42. Bought 10. Under your $500 cap."

**Never:** "get rich", urgency or FOMO, "set it and forget it" (forgetting is the failure mode).

## 5. Marketing angle

Don't lead with the product ("Robinhood has agents now"). Lead with the tension the audience
already feels.

- **Apprentice line:** "Don't learn the market alone." / "Your first trade, with someone watching
  the market 24/7."
- **Operator line:** "You've done this a thousand times. Teach it once." / "Your playbook, running
  while you sleep. On your terms."
- **Brand line (both):** "Take the chance. Keep the leash."

Campaign idea: show the **leash** literally. Every spot ends on the same frame: the agent working,
the limit meter, and a thumb resting on stop.

## 6. Name directions (trademark check needed)

Pull from the Robin Hood legend so the endorsement feels native.

- **Little John.** Robin Hood's right-hand man: the trusted, capable sidekick. Fits both personas;
  the irony of the name (he's huge) gives it charm.
- **Quiver.** A set of arrows ready to fire. Agents as strategies you load and release.
- **Sherwood.** The world outside the conventional system. Strong mood, but it's a place, not a
  companion, so it's weaker for the relationship idea.

Recommendation: **Little John, by Robinhood.**

## 7. How this fits the UI system

1. **It repairs the reuse claim.** ADR-0003 made the library a literal Robinhood copy, which weakened
   reuse at the visual layer. Now the same behavioral components render under **two brands**: the
   Robinhood theme (baseline, parent) and the sub-brand theme. Then travel and email. Token
   architecture: neutral core → parent theme → sub-brand theme.
2. **Persona = mode, not new components.** Apprentice and Operator are two configurations of the
   same mandate: different default autonomy, different explanation density, different onboarding
   path. If a persona needs a new component, that's a finding.
3. **Earned autonomy is a machine.** Autonomy expanding as trust is shown belongs in
   `mandateMachine`, not in the UI.
4. **The look is behavioral tokens.** Loosen and tighten, motion, uncertainty texture: these are
   `behavior/*` tokens (§9.5), not decoration.

## 8. Risks to resolve

1. **Branding confound in the study.** The brief keeps both conditions in identical branding so
   participants react to design, not brand. If the system condition is sub-branded, that breaks.
   **Proposal:** test behavior in one shared theme (as planned). Test the sub-brand separately, as a
   concept and desirability study with each persona.
2. **Mentoring edges into advice.** An agent that "guides" the Apprentice looks like an investment
   recommendation. Robinhood explicitly says it doesn't "recommend" agents' trades. The
   Apprentice mode needs a clear line between explaining and recommending. Flag it; don't solve it
   with copy alone.
3. **Scope.** A full brand identity is a lot of work. Keep the sub-brand at **concept + core
   identity** (name, principles, voice, the behavioral visual language, one campaign frame). The
   contribution is still the behavioral system.
4. **Research questions.** RQ1/RQ2 and RQ3 need a persona dimension: does comprehension and
   detection differ between Apprentice and Operator?

## 9. Decisions for the owner

1. Sub-brand as an **endorsed brand** ("by Robinhood")?
2. Personas: **Apprentice** and **Operator**, with the Cautious Holder left to the parent brand?
3. Core idea: **"undefined, but held"** / "Take the chance. Keep the leash."?
4. Name direction: Little John, Quiver, Sherwood, or another?
5. Accept the study split: behavior tested in a shared theme, brand tested separately?
